7.1 Everything was KCL and KVL

Look back at what each part actually added.

PartWhat it introducedWhat it really was
1KCL, KVL, Ohm, powerconservation of charge and of energy
2series, parallel, dividersone line of KVL and one of KCL, pre-solved
3node and mesh analysisthe same two laws written at every node or loop at once
4Thévenin and Nortonthe same two laws, solved once and reused
5RMS and waveform valuesthe number that makes AC power obey the DC formulas
6phasors and impedancethe same two laws, over complex numbers

Not one of those parts introduced a new law of physics. Parts 2 to 4 are bookkeeping, chosen to make the laws cheap to apply. Parts 5 and 6 are a change of number system that lets the whole DC toolkit survive contact with a sinusoid. If you understand Part 1 and can do complex arithmetic, you can rederive every other formula in this module.

7.3 The cheat sheet

PartWhat to have in memory
1V=IR; P = VI = I²R = V²/R; KCL and KVL as sums to zero
1Current enters a resistor at its + terminal. A negative result means the arrow was drawn backwards, not that the work was wrong.
2Series adds R; parallel adds 1/R. Product over sum for two. R/n for n equal.
2Voltage divider: own R on top. Current divider: the other R on top.
3Conductance matrix by inspection: diagonal is what touches the node, off-diagonal is minus what is shared.
3Count nodes − 1 against meshes before choosing a method.
4V_th = V_oc, R_th = V_oc/I_sc. Kill sources: voltage → short, current → open.
4Max power at R_L = R_th, worth V_th²/4R_th, at 50% efficiency.
5ω = 2πf; 60 Hz is 377 rad/s; Vrms = Vm/√2 for sinusoids only.
5A quoted AC voltage is RMS; an equation's coefficient is peak. Power always uses RMS.
6Z_L = jωL, Z_C = −j/ωC; ELI the ICE man.
6P = VI cos θ, Q = VI sin θ, S² = P² + Q². Power factor always carries lagging or leading.

7.4 On exam day, for this topic specifically

  • Label the nodes before anything else. Most wrong answers in circuit analysis come from misreading the topology, not from the arithmetic. Trace each wire with a finger and mark which junctions are actually the same node.
  • Check with the law you did not use. Solved by mesh? Check KCL at a node. Solved by node? Check KVL round a loop. Then check power balance. Three free verifications, ten seconds each.
  • Watch the RMS/peak boundary. It is the single most common source of a √2 error, and both wrong values will be among the options.
  • Say lagging or leading. A bare power factor is an incomplete answer and the exam grades it as one.
  • Sanity-check magnitudes. A parallel combination smaller than its smallest member; the easier path carrying more current; a power factor between 0 and 1. Each of these strikes out options for free.

7.5 Where to go next

  • Linear Systems (5–8 questions) takes Part 6's impedance and lets the frequency vary — resonance is where X_L = X_C and the reactance cancels. Transient response is Mathematics Part 7's differential equations applied to the same RLC circuit.
  • Power Systems (8–12) extends Part 6's power triangle to three phases, transformers and transmission lines. Power factor correction is the direct sequel to Plate 35.
  • Electronics (7–11) replaces resistors with devices whose V–I curves are not straight lines — but the analysis around them is still Parts 1 to 4, applied to a small-signal model.
  • Signal Processing and Communications (10–16) is Part 6 across many frequencies at once, which is what a Fourier transform is for.

Two conservation laws, one component rule, and a change of number system cover the whole of Circuit Analysis. Everything else in this module exists to make those cheap to apply, and every one of them can be rederived if it slips.

And the triangle from the first plate of the Mathematics module turned out to be an impedance, a current lag, and a power factor. That was not a coincidence arranged for the guide — it is why the exam keeps using those numbers.
