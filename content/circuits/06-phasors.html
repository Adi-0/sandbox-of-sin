Phasors and Impedance

Ohm's law, with an angle. Every method from Parts 1 to 4 survives intact — the only change is that resistance becomes a complex number, and the 3-4-5 triangle arrives to be that number.

6.1 The trade that makes AC easy

Mathematics Part 2 built the machinery; this is what it was for. A phasor is a complex number holding a sinusoid's amplitude and phase, on the understanding that everything in the circuit shares one frequency. Once the frequency is agreed, it need never be written down again.

v(t) = Vm cos(ωt+φ) ↔ V = Vrms∠φ

Trap · RMS or peak in the phasor?

Both conventions exist. Power engineering — and the FE exam — uses RMS magnitudes, so that P = VI cos θ comes out right with no stray factor of two. Some textbooks use peak. If a problem quotes a bare voltage, treat it as RMS; if it hands you a time-domain equation, convert the peak first.

6.2 Impedance: resistance that can rotate

A resistor obeys v = iR — voltage and current in step. An inductor obeys v = L di/dt, and a capacitor i = C dv/dt. Those derivatives are what made AC circuits differential equations.

But differentiating a sinusoid is the same as multiplying its phasor by jω — a 90° rotation and a scaling. So the derivative disappears, and every component gets a single complex number relating its voltage phasor to its current phasor. That number is its impedance.

ELI the ICE man

The standard mnemonic, and it does earn its keep. In an inductor (L), E leads I — voltage first. In a capacitor (C), I leads E — current first. Physically: a capacitor must be charged before its voltage rises, so current comes first; an inductor resists a change in current, so voltage appears before the current responds.

6.3 The cast arrives

Take a resistor of 3 Ω in series with an inductive reactance of 4 Ω. The impedance is

Z = 3 + j4 = 5∠53.13° Ω

which is exactly the complex number from Mathematics Part 2 and the triangle from Mathematics Part 1. This is not a teaching device borrowed for the occasion — the impedance triangle really is a right triangle, with resistance along one leg, reactance along the other, and magnitude as the hypotenuse. Every FE textbook uses these numbers for the same reason.

Drive it with 100 V and the current follows in one division:

I = 20∠−53.13° A

20 A, lagging the voltage by 53.13° — lagging because the load is inductive, as ELI promised. Compare the effort with the trigonometric route: this is one division.

6.4 Power in an AC circuit

Now a complication with no DC counterpart. Current lags voltage, so for part of every cycle they have opposite signs and the instantaneous power is negative — energy is flowing back out of the load into the source. The reactance stores it and returns it, over and over, without ever consuming any.

So AC power needs three numbers, not one.

The cast, one last time

With V = 100 V and I = 20 A at 53.13°:

S = 2000 VA, P = 1200 W, Q = 1600 VAR

Check: 1200² + 1600² = 2000². The power triangle is the 3-4-5 triangle, scaled by 400. The same three numbers that were a triangle in Mathematics Part 1, a complex number in Part 2, an eigenvalue in Part 5 and a characteristic root in Part 7 are now watts, VARs and volt-amperes.

Two cross-checks worth knowing, because they catch nearly every slip: P = I²R using only the resistive part gives 400 × 3 = 1200 W ✓, and Q = I²X gives 400 × 4 = 1600 VAR ✓. Real power comes only from resistance; reactive power comes only from reactance.

Trap · power factor without a direction is half an answer

A power factor of 0.6 could be an inductive load at +53.13° or a capacitive one at −53.13°; the cosine cannot tell them apart. A complete answer is always "0.6 lagging" or "0.6 leading" — lagging for inductive, leading for capacitive. Exam options routinely include both, and only one is right.

6.5 What to memorise from this part

Know cold

  • Z_L = jωL and Z_C = −j/ωC. Inductive reactance rises with frequency; capacitive falls.
  • Z = R + jX, and impedances combine exactly like resistances.
  • ELI the ICE man — voltage leads current in an inductor, lags it in a capacitor.
  • P = VI cos θ, Q = VI sin θ, S = VI, and S² = P² + Q².
  • Power factor always carries lagging or leading.
  • The cast: 3 + j4 = 5∠53.13°, giving 1200 W, 1600 VAR, 2000 VA at 0.6 lagging.

A phasor stores a sinusoid's amplitude and phase once the frequency is agreed. Impedance is the complex ratio of voltage phasor to current phasor, and because KCL and KVL never stopped holding, every technique from Parts 1 to 4 carries over untouched — only the arithmetic becomes complex.

AC power splits into a part that is consumed and a part that is merely borrowed, and the two combine as the legs of a right triangle whose angle is the impedance angle. It is the same triangle, for the fifth time.
