Linear Algebra

A matrix is not a table of numbers. It is a machine that moves space — and once you see it that way, the determinant stops being a computation and becomes a diagnosis.

5.1 Why matrices exist

Node analysis on a five-node circuit gives you five equations in five unknowns. Solving them by substitution is possible and miserable. A matrix is the bookkeeping that lets you write all five at once and then operate on the whole system as a single object.

the system, and the same system as a matrix equation

The right-hand version is Ax = b, and it looks exactly like the scalar equation ax = b — which is the point. Everything you want to do to a system of equations, you can now do to one letter.

5.2 Multiplication, and why order matters

Matrix multiplication is defined so that Ax produces exactly the left-hand sides of your equations. Entry (i,j) of the product is the dot product of row i of the first matrix with column j of the second — the same dot product from Part 4, not a new operation.

5.3 A matrix moves space

Here is the idea that makes the rest of this part easy. Feed a matrix every point in the plane and it moves them all — and it moves them consistently: straight lines stay straight, the origin stays put, parallel lines stay parallel. Every matrix is a stretch, a shear, a rotation, a reflection, or some combination.

So look at what it does to the unit square. Turn the knob in Plate 14 and watch the square deform.

The area of what the square becomes is the determinant. That is not an analogy — it is what the determinant is.

5.4 Singular means "no unique answer"

If a matrix flattens the plane onto a line, then many different starting points land on the same place — and you cannot work backwards from a result to a unique cause. Such a matrix is singular: its determinant is zero, it has no inverse, and the system Ax = b has either no solution or infinitely many, never exactly one.

Geometrically, in two dimensions, that is two lines that are parallel instead of crossing. Plate 15 shows the three cases side by side.

Worked · Cramer's rule, end to end

Solve 2x + 3y = 8 and x − y = 1.

  1. The determinant of the coefficient matrix, first — it is the go/no-go check: |A| = −5 Non-zero, so there is exactly one solution.
  2. Replace the first column with the right-hand side to get x: x = 2.2
  3. Replace the second column for y: y = 1.2 Check in the second equation: 2.2 − 1.2 = 1 ✓

Always substitute back into the equation you did not use to build the numerator. It costs five seconds and catches sign errors, which are the only errors this method really produces.

5.5 Eigenvectors: the directions a matrix leaves alone

A matrix moves almost every vector to a new direction. But most matrices have a few special directions that come out pointing the same way they went in — only longer or shorter. Those are the eigenvectors, and the stretch factor for each is its eigenvalue.

Av = λv

Read that as a sentence: the matrix acting on this vector does nothing but scale it. Turn the knob in Plate 16 and hunt for the directions where the two arrows line up.

The cast, as a matrix

Take A = [[3,4],[4,−3]]. Its trace is 0 and its determinant is −25. So the eigenvalues must sum to 0 and multiply to −25 — they are ±5, without solving anything.

The 3-4-5 triangle's hypotenuse, arriving as an eigenvalue. And the negative one is not a defect: this matrix is a reflection scaled by 5, so one direction is stretched and the perpendicular one is flipped.

Trap · what the exam actually asks

Most FE eigenvalue questions give you a small matrix and four candidate value-pairs. You almost never need to solve the characteristic equation — check each option against the trace and the determinant and usually only one survives. Two multiplications beats a quadratic every time.

5.6 What to memorise from this part

Know cold

  • The 2×2 determinant, ad − bc, and that it is an area scale factor.
  • det A = 0 means singular: no inverse, no unique solution.
  • The shape rule for multiplication, and that AB ≠ BA.
  • The 2×2 inverse: swap the diagonal, negate the off-diagonal, divide by the determinant.
  • Av = λv as a sentence, not a formula.
  • Trace = sum of eigenvalues, determinant = product. This is the fastest check on the exam.

A matrix is a machine that moves space consistently, and its determinant is the factor by which it scales area. Zero determinant means space was flattened, which is why singular matrices have no inverse and their systems have no unique solution.

Eigenvectors are the directions the machine leaves pointing the same way; the eigenvalue is how much it stretched them. For a 2×2, the eigenvalues sum to the trace and multiply to the determinant — check answers with that before you solve anything.
